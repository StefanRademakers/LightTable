Task 213 - Repair Color and Vibrance Temperature/Tint parity evidence and model

Goal

Replace the unsupported CAT16 Temperature/Tint implementation with one model
that matches the measured Photoshop Color and Vibrance behavior on rendered
RGB images, while preserving the independently good Vibrance and Saturation
implementation.

Why this task exists

Task 211 removed 6.14 MB of shipped calibration data successfully, but its
acceptance evidence was incomplete. The reported 99.253% diagnostic covered
only positive Vibrance and Saturation on one portrait. Temperature and Tint
were both zero in every case used for that number.

A complete 40-case portrait comparison against Photoshop Beta 27.11 shows:

- Temperature: 18.462% mean RGB RMSE; 33.001% at -100.
- Tint: 8.223% mean RGB RMSE; 15.960% at +100.
- Vibrance: 2.053% mean RGB RMSE across signed cases.
- Saturation: 0.292% mean RGB RMSE across signed cases.

The percentage formerly called parity was `100 * (1 - normalized RGB RMSE)`.
It was not a perceptual score or the percentage of matching pixels and must not
be used as a complete Color and Vibrance claim.

Measured model facts

- Photoshop keeps exact black and white fixed while strongly tinting shadows
  and midtones at Temperature/Tint extremes.
- Temperature and Tint are coupled; axis deltas and sequential axis transforms
  both fail badly on the measured 21 x 21 grid.
- On the same JPEG portrait, Photoshop Color and Vibrance and Camera Raw 18.5
  have 0.99986-0.99998 effect correlation and 0.99979-1.00018 effect magnitude
  ratio at signed Temperature/Tint endpoints. Treat them as the same rendered-
  pixel behavior unless later evidence disproves that boundary.
- Adobe documents -100..100 Temperature for JPEG/TIFF as an approximation,
  not a true Kelvin scale. Do not map this control directly to CCT and call it
  parity.

Acceptance

- Keep the exact neutral bypass.
- Do not change Vibrance or Saturation response while repairing Temperature
  and Tint.
- Establish provenance-bound Photoshop Beta and Camera Raw endpoint evidence.
- Cover neutral, signed 20/80/100 single controls, combined signed/crossed
  white-balance cases, held-out intermediate values, a color lattice, smooth
  gradients, and at least two photographs.
- Report RGB RMSE/MAE, maximum code error, threshold coverage and perceptual
  deltas per case. Do not collapse four sliders into one parity percentage.
- Reject a candidate with severe local errors even when its aggregate improves.
- Reuse a shared rendered-pixel Temperature/Tint core with native Grade when
  the semantics match; keep RAW camera white balance separate.
- Keep shipped calibration/model data materially below the removed 6.14 MB and
  justify any remaining data by visible and held-out improvement.
- Exercise packaged Electron/WebGPU output before completion.

Current state

Research and safe capture infrastructure are in progress. No production shader
change has been accepted yet. Task 211's bundle reduction and Vibrance/
Saturation work remain useful; its full-node parity implication is withdrawn.
