Task 211 - Replace the brute-force Color and Vibrance calibration corpus

Goal

Replace the shipped Photoshop 27 calibration corpus with a compact,
explainable color model that remains useful at slider extremes and explicitly
protects skin-like colors and output gamut.

Why this task exists

The old implementation embedded 441 complete 13^3 RGB white-balance volumes
and 49 complete color volumes. Its generated TypeScript was 8.59 MB and held
6.14 MB of binary calibration data. No LightTable documents have been shipped,
so preserving that alpha implementation is not a product requirement.

Decision

Treat the captures as research and regression evidence, not as the product
contract. Use CAT16 white-point adaptation for Temperature/Tint and an OKLab
chroma model for Vibrance/Saturation. Make low-saturation response, soft
OKLCH skin likelihood and gamut projection explicit and testable.

Acceptance

- Remove all 490 Color and Vibrance volumes from shipped source.
- Remove its generated data from initial JavaScript and per-layer GPU textures.
- Preserve an exact neutral bypass.
- Keep Temperature/Tint coupled as a white-point operation.
- Protect muted colors, a broad skin-like region and out-of-gamut results.
- Test neutral, signed 20/80/100 values, combined signed 80 extremes, smooth
  gradients and isolated portrait Vibrance/Saturation cases.
- Measure the production web bundle before and after.
- Keep Photoshop capture tooling available as research infrastructure without
  making it a production dependency.

Do not

- Claim the analytic model is Adobe's private implementation.
- Tune only to one aggregate Photoshop score.
- Use hard RGB clipping or a coarse iterative gamut boundary.
- Call a broad chromatic mask semantic person detection.
